**Abstract:** Deep generative models have attracted considerable attention as inference engines for stochastic model updating. Most toxicity phenotypes are routinely evaluated during peptide development including cytotoxicity, hemolysis, neurotoxicity, embryotoxicity, and ichthyotoxicity (19,27). These toxicity endpoints have direct implications for efficacy, safety, dosage, and regulatory approval. Toxicity annotations have consequently accumulated across public databases (31,18,24), literature-derived resources (26,29,28,34), supplementary datasets (9,4,23), and experimental studies (33,39). However, these resources remain fragmented, employ heterogeneous terminology and annotation criteria, and provide variable levels of metadata and evidence quality (21). Their development for distinct biological objectives further complicates integration, reproducibility, and large-scale analysis (17).The growing availability of toxicity data creates opportunities for computational toxicology, data-driven peptide engineering, and large-scale biological analyses (32). Their utility, however, depends on the quality, consistency, and transparency of the underlying data (14,7). Annotation ambiguity, conflicting evidence, heterogeneous endpoint definitions, incomplete metadata, and limited provenance tracking can reduce reproducibility and complicate downstream reuse (1). The FAIR principles-Findability, Accessibility, Interoperability, and Reusability-provide a framework for organizing and distributing scientific data to support discovery, integration, reproducibility, and long-term reuse (22). **Abstract:** This work addresses electric vehicle (EV) charging station placement through a bi-level optimization model, where the upper-level planner maximizes charging revenue by selecting station locations under budget constraints, while EV users at the lower level choose routes and charging stations to minimize travel and charging costs. To account for battery range limitation, we construct a battery-expanded network and apply a shortest path algorithm with Frank-Wolfe traffic assignment. Our primary contribution is developing the first global solution algorithm for large scale EV charging station placement problems. We propose a Branch-and-Price-and-Cut algorithm enhanced with value function cuts and column generation. Our exact algorithm delivers solutions provably within the reported optimality gap of the global optimum with mathematical certainty. **Abstract:** Abstract Public-sector building retrofits in the United Kingdom require decisions that balance whole-life cost, carbon reduction, service continuity and auditability under limited data and constrained capital budgets. **Abstract:** Ecological theory predicts that local species richness can influence biogeographic rates of speciation, extinction, and dispersal. For instance, increasing the number of competing species within a region may cause local speciation and dispersal rates to decrease but local extinction rates to increase, inducing a carrying capacity for local species richness. In this article, we introduce a fully generative, event-based phylogenetic diversification model, called DDGeoSSE, that allows diversity-dependent effects of local species richness to modulate biogeographic rates of diversification and range evolution. DDGeoSSE can accommodate and test a variety of alternative diversification scenarios that involve positive, negative, and neutral interactions among sympatric species for speciation, extinction, and dispersal. We derive mathematical and statistical properties of biogeographic outcomes generated by this model, such as the carrying capacity for a clade at equilibrium, which we validate through simulation. Because diversity-dependent phylogenetic models typically do not have tractable likelihood functions, we use deep learning with \texttt{phyddle} to perform parameter inference and model selection. Separately applying DDGeoSSE to Caribbean \textit{Anolis} lizards and cloud forest-dwelling \textit{Viburnum} plants, we find evidence that local species richness plays a significant role in shaping diversification dynamics for both clades.

**Abstract:** Translating developmental time across species is essential for the appropriate design and interpretation of preclinical neurodevelopmental research, yet no open, unified resource currently integrates high-quality developmental milestone data for mouse, rat, and human. Here we present a curated, open-access dataset of general and neurodevelopmental milestones spanning these three species, expanding a previously published rat--human database with newly compiled mouse data. Following a systematic literature search, 783 records covering 371 unique developmental milestones were extracted, standardized to days after fertilization, and validated through a structured curation and quality-control process. Exploratory analyses indicated that the developmental relationship between humans and rodents is markedly non-linear, with milestones becoming increasingly dispersed as development progresses.
