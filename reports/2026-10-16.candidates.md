**Abstract:** The Practical Molecular Optimization (PMO) benchmark standardized evaluation in molecular optimization, but it is built on simple property-based oracles that do not capture structure-based drug design. As the field has moved to docking-based objectives, evaluation practice has become unstandardized, and different methods report results on different docking tasks, often without a shared protocol for fair comparison or sample-efficiency constraints. To address this, we introduce Practical Molecular Optimization for Docking (PMO-Dock), a benchmark and protocol for docking-based optimization consisting of 25 tasks covering hit generation, lead optimization, and a new specificity task requiring strong on-target binding while penalizing off-target interactions. The protocol separates model development from final evaluation: validation tasks are used for hyperparameter tuning, and test tasks are held out for final evaluation and are not used during development. Our results show no universally best method across tasks, additional analyses of hyperparameter transfer (cross-task and cross-category) and sensitivity show that transfer is often limited and varies by method, motivating method-dependent selection strategies. This benchmark provides a concrete standard for evaluating generalization to unseen tasks and sample efficiency under a limited test-time oracle budget.

**Abstract:** Deep generative models have attracted considerable attention as inference engines for stochastic model updating. Among these deep generative models, conditional flow matching offers a particularly efficient formulation, in which a continuous transport map from a latent distribution to the posterior is learned by regression against optimal-transport probability paths, avoiding both the invertibility constraints of normalising flows and the iterative denoising of diffusion models. However, its performance in engineering model updating has not been examined against an established baseline such as the Bayesian updating framework under controlled conditions. In this work, conditional flow matching is therefore assessed against the Bayesian model updating approach with transitional Markov chain Monte Carlo, in which the Bhattacharyya distance provides the approximate likelihood for the Bayesian approach. **Abstract:** Representation-alignment methods effectively accelerate the diffusion transformer training. REPA, for example, uses a pretrained vision encoder to supervise an intermediate layer through an MLP projection head which is discarded after training. It aims to import semantics from the vision encoder to the diffusion backbone. While this objective is achieved at early layers by back-propagating the alignment loss, there is no guarantee that later layers, supervised by the velocity prediction loss alone, can well access such semantics. We propose a simple solution, representation conditioning (RECON), to improve semantics in later layers. During training, we feed the pretrained vision encoder tokens into later layers; during inference, because we no longer have the clean image, we reuse the MLP head (as a proxy to the vision encoder) and project its output tokens to the later layers. **Abstract:** Variational quantum optimization provides a useful setting for examining how circuit structure, parameterization, and trainability interact in practice. Objectives: Among the most promising variational quantum algorithms, the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) depends critically on circuit architecture, but greater circuit complexity does not necessarily lead to easier optimization or better solutions. Using the Maximum Cut (MaxCut) problem on graphs as a controlled setting, we investigate how variational behavior changes as problem-specific QAOA structure is progressively removed, with particular attention to representational capacity, optimization geometry, and finite-budget trainability. Methods: We analytically characterize a graph-agnostic product-state formulation of MaxCut and derive exact equivalences, redundancies, and local-landscape properties. These results motivate a controlled comparison among separable product ansätze, a representative graphagnostic entangling circuit, and graph-structured QAOA at several depths, including a parameter-count-matched control. The numerical benchmark comprises 95 small unweighted graph instances optimized under a common objective-evaluation budget. Results: The product-state formulation preserves the exact discrete MaxCut optimum, while its strict local maxima correspond to classical cuts that are stable under singlevertex flips. Redundant phase parameters increase optimization effort without enlarging the objective-relevant family. Under the prescribed optimization budget, the productstate ansatz attains higher average solution quality than the tested QAOA depths while requiring substantially fewer objective evaluations. The graph-agnostic entangling comparator provides no consistent advantage over the separable baseline, and a simple classical local-search method remains highly competitive. Conclusion: In the regime considered here, objective-relevant parameterization and optimization geometry can be as important as circuit richness.
